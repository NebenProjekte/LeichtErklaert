Biology - Microbiology

Bacteria Explained Simply

Bacteria are tiny, single-celled organisms - unlike viruses, they're clearly alive: they have their own metabolism and can reproduce entirely on their own.

1 cell → 2 cells → 4 cells → 8 cells ... bacteria reproduce through simple cell division - under good conditions, their numbers can double every 20 minutes

As simple as this splitting looks - it's exactly why bacteria can multiply so rapidly in a short time.

💬 Mia asks Grandpa Theo

Mia

Aren't bacteria just disease-causing germs?

Grandpa Theo

Quite the opposite! Only a small fraction of bacteria make you sick. Most are harmless or even genuinely helpful - your gut alone hosts trillions of bacteria that help you digest food.

Mia

What's actually different about them compared to our own cells?

Grandpa Theo

Our cells have a nucleus that encloses the genetic material - bacterial cells don't. In them, the genetic material just floats freely inside the cell. That's why bacteria are called "prokaryotes", meaning "before the nucleus".

Mia

And why do antibiotics eventually stop working properly?

Grandpa Theo

Because bacteria multiply so fast, random mutations also pop up quickly. If one happens to protect against an antibiotic, that particular bacterium survives the treatment and keeps reproducing - a direct example of natural selection.

Not just in the body

Bacteria are also in yogurt, cheese, and sauerkraut - there they deliberately turn milk sugar or cabbage into lactic acid, which changes taste and shelf life.

That's why it's important to always finish a full course of antibiotics: stopping too early leaves exactly the toughest, most resistant bacteria behind to survive.
