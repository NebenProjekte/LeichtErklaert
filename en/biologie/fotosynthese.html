Biology - Plant Biology

Photosynthesis Explained Simply

Photosynthesis is the process plants use to make their own energy from light, water, and carbon dioxide. It doesn't happen in a single step - it runs in two stages that build on each other: the light reactions and the Calvin cycle.

Light reactions → ATP + NADPH → Calvin cycle → glucose the light reactions need direct light and supply energy, the Calvin cycle uses that energy to build sugar from CO₂

Only the light reactions need direct light - the Calvin cycle just uses the energy they produce, so it can keep running briefly even in the dark.

💬 Mia asks Grandpa Theo

Mia

Why does the plant even need two separate steps? Why not just do it all at once?

Grandpa Theo

Because they're two completely different jobs. In the light reactions, light energy gets captured and converted into two "energy carriers" - ATP and NADPH. Those energy carriers are then used in the Calvin cycle to build sugar molecules from carbon dioxide, step by step. Two different tools for two different tasks.

Mia

Where does the oxygen we breathe actually come from - is it from the CO₂?

Grandpa Theo

Surprisingly, no! The oxygen comes from the water, not the CO₂. In the light reactions, water gets split apart to supply electrons for the energy carriers - oxygen is basically a "waste product" of that, which the plant releases into the air.

Mia

So does a plant grow faster and faster the more light it gets?

Grandpa Theo

Only up to a point. Photosynthesis is always limited by whichever "ingredient" is scarcest - sometimes that's light, sometimes the amount of CO₂ in the air, sometimes temperature. If you double the light but CO₂ is still lacking, it barely helps at all. This is called the principle of limiting factors.

You can measure it

In aquarium plants like pondweed, you can literally count the rate of photosynthesis: the more light, the more tiny oxygen bubbles rise from the leaves per minute - a classic school experiment on limiting factors.

Almost every food chain on Earth starts with photosynthesis - plants and algae are the foundation that practically every other living thing depends on, directly or indirectly. The reverse process is cellular respiration, which releases that stored energy again.
