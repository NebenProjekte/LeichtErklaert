Chromosomes Explained Simply

A chromosome is the tightly packed form in which your DNA exists in every cell. If the DNA were not wound up so tightly, it would never fit into the tiny cell nucleus.

23 pairs = 46 chromosomes humans have 46 chromosomes in almost every cell - 23 from the mother, 23 from the father

One chromosome pair - one inherited from each parent. Humans have 23 such pairs.

💬 Mia asks Grandpa Theo

Mia

What exactly is the difference between a chromosome, DNA and a gene? It all sounds the same to me.

Grandpa Theo

Think of a book: the DNA is the whole text. A gene is a single chapter with one particular set of instructions. And a chromosome is a whole bound volume that contains many genes one after another.

Mia

Why are they always pairs and not single chromosomes?

Grandpa Theo

Because you get one copy of each chromosome from your mother and one from your father. For every one of your 23 chromosomes you therefore have two versions - a maternal one and a paternal one.

Mia

Is one of the 23 pairs somehow special?

Grandpa Theo

Yes, pair number 23 - the sex chromosomes. In the other 22 pairs the two chromosomes look very similar, but in the 23rd pair the combination of X and X or X and Y decides whether egg cells or sperm cells are produced.

When the count is off

Sometimes an error occurs during cell division and a chromosome is present three times instead of twice. When this happens with chromosome 21, it is called trisomy 21 or Down syndrome - a well-known example of how strongly even a single extra chromosome can affect development.

Doctors can make a person's chromosomes visible under the microscope and sort them by size. This is called a karyogram, and it allows some genetic conditions to be recognised directly.

Key terms

Chromosome — tightly packed DNA, visible under the microscope only during nuclear division.
Chromatid — one of the two identical halves of a duplicated chromosome.
Centromere — the constriction where the chromatids are joined.
Homologous chromosomes — the maternal and paternal copy of a pair.
Karyogram — the ordered display of all chromosomes by size.
Diploid / haploid — double set (46) in body cells, single set (23) in sex cells.

Of the 23 pairs, 22 are so-called autosomes; the 23rd pair determines sex: XX or XY. Each pair consists of a maternal and a paternal chromosome with the same gene loci, although the versions of the genes they carry can differ.

Why halving is necessary

If two body cells with 46 chromosomes each were to fuse, the next generation would have 92 and the one after that 184. That is why meiosis halves the set before fertilisation. On the side, it also reshuffles the deck: the pairs are distributed at random, and sections are exchanged between homologous chromosomes. The random distribution alone gives 2²³, or more than eight million possibilities.

How a chromosome is copied and distributed

Before a cell divides, it duplicates all of its DNA. Each chromosome then becomes a double structure made of two identical chromatids that are joined at the centromere. Only now does the familiar X shape appear. In mitosis, spindle fibres pull on the centromeres and separate the two chromatids from each other. Each daughter cell thus receives exactly one complete copy of every chromosome and has 46 chromosomes again. If something goes wrong with the distribution in mitosis or meiosis, a cell ends up with one chromosome too many or too few. Trisomy 21 arises in this way.

Chromosome numbers compared

The number of chromosomes says nothing about how complex an organism is. The fruit fly has 8, the pea 14, the cat 38, humans 46, the chimpanzee 48 and the dog 78 chromosomes. There is a well-known reason why humans and chimpanzees differ by two: in our ancestors, two medium-sized chromosomes fused into a single one. It is human chromosome 2.

Common mistakes

Equating chromosome and DNA. The chromosome is the packaging form.
Always drawing chromosomes as an X. They only have this shape after duplication; most of the time they lie relaxed and invisible.
"More chromosomes means more advanced." A fern has over 1,200, humans have 46.
Mixing up haploid and diploid. Sex cells are haploid, body cells diploid.

Practice task

Explain why siblings resemble one another but are not identical, even though they have the same parents. Name two mechanisms.

Show solution

First, the random distribution of the chromosome pairs in meiosis: for each of the 23 pairs, chance decides whether the maternal or the paternal copy ends up in the sex cell. That gives 2²³ = 8,388,608 combinations per parent.

Second, crossing over: homologous chromosomes exchange sections before they separate, which produces entirely new gene combinations on a single chromosome.

Together with the random choice of which sperm cell reaches the egg cell, this results in more than 70 trillion possible combinations. Siblings share on average 50% of their genes - but never the same 50%. Only identical twins are an exception.
