Biology - Evolution

Darwin and the Theory of Evolution Explained Simply

In the 19th century, Charles Darwin described how living things change over very long periods of time and how new species arise. The core mechanism behind this is called natural selection.

Variation + selection + inheritance = evolution random differences between individuals, a survival advantage resulting from those differences, and passing them on to the offspring

The size of the circles stands for small changes that add up over very many generations - not for a single big leap.

💬 Mia asks Grandpa Theo

Mia

Does "survival of the fittest" mean that the strongest animals always win?

Grandpa Theo

No, that is a big misunderstanding! It means "best adapted", not "strongest". A moth that is well camouflaged in the dark wins against predators, not because it fights, but because it simply gets overlooked.

Mia

But how do the differences between animals arise in the first place?

Grandpa Theo

Through small random changes in the genetic material, called mutations. Most of them do not matter, some are harmful - but every now and then one happens to be useful. Animals with the useful trait have, on average, more offspring that survive, and the trait becomes more common in the population.

Mia

And at some point that turns into a completely new species?

Grandpa Theo

If two groups of one species develop separately for long enough - for example on different islands - so many small differences can accumulate that they can eventually no longer reproduce with each other. Then they have become two separate species. That is exactly what Darwin observed in the finches of the Galápagos Islands.

Not just the past

Evolution still happens today, and it can be measured: within a few years, bacteria develop resistance to antibiotics, because resistant bacteria survive and multiply - an example of natural selection in real time.

Important: evolution has no goal and no "striving" to become perfect - it is simply the result of randomly useful traits becoming established in a population.

Key terms

Variation — individuals of a species differ from one another.
Selection — the environment favours certain variants.
Adaptation — the result, not a goal and not an intention.
Mutation — a random change in the genetic material, the raw material of evolution.
Recombination — reshuffling of genes during sexual reproduction.
Fitness — not strength, but reproductive success.

The process in four steps

How evolution works
StepWhat happens
1Within a species there are random differences
2More offspring are born than can survive
3Those who fit the environment better have, on average, more offspring
4The traits of these individuals become more common - the species changes

The order is crucial: the variation is there first and arises by chance. The environment creates nothing; it only selects. The common phrase "the animal adapted" twists exactly this.

Evidence that fits together independently

Fossils show transitional forms such as Archaeopteryx, with feathers and teeth. Comparative anatomy finds the same bone plan in the human arm, the bat wing and the whale flipper. The embryonic development of all vertebrates is strikingly similar. And molecular genetics arranges species by their DNA into the same family trees that had already been derived from body structure. Four methods that are independent of each other, one result.

Selection you can observe: the peppered moth

The peppered moth is a night-flying moth that rests on tree bark during the day. There are light and dark forms. On light, lichen-covered trunks the light moths are hard to see, while the dark ones stand out and are eaten more often by birds. In the 19th century, soot from industry blackened the trunks in English cities, and the balance tipped: dark moths became common there, light ones rare. When the air later became cleaner again, the development reversed. The variation was already there beforehand; the environment only changed which form had the advantage.

Survival is not everything: sexual selection

Darwin realised that the choice of a mate also shapes traits. The long tail of the peacock is more of a hindrance when fleeing, but it improves his chances with the peahens. A trait therefore becomes established if it brings more offspring on average, even if it shortens life. That is exactly what fitness means: not strength or a long life, but reproductive success.

Common mistakes

"Humans descend from apes." Humans and the great apes share common ancestors - that is something different.
Evolution as intention. No living thing decides to develop a trait.
"Survival of the fittest means the strongest." It means the best adapted - often that is the less conspicuous one.
Reading "theory" as a guess. In the natural sciences, a theory is an explanatory framework supported by many findings.

Practice task

Use antibiotic-resistant bacteria as an example to explain the four steps of evolution. Why is the phrase "the bacteria adapted" misleading?

Show solution

1. In every large bacterial population, mutation randomly produces individual cells with altered target structures.
2. Bacteria multiply far beyond the carrying capacity.
3. When an antibiotic is added, the sensitive cells die and the resistant ones survive.
4. The next generation descends almost entirely from the resistant ones - their share rises sharply.

The phrase is misleading because no bacterium reacted to the antibiotic. The resistance was present beforehand, by chance. The antibiotic did not create it, it only picked it out.
