Viruses Explained Simply

A virus is tiny - much smaller than a cell - and basically consists of just genetic material wrapped in a protein shell. The surprising part: viruses usually aren't considered "alive".

Genetic material + protein shell = virus without its own metabolism, a virus can't reproduce on its own - it absolutely needs a host cell to do that

The spikes (red) are docking proteins the virus uses to attach to a matching cell - like a key that only fits certain locks.

💬 Mia asks Grandpa Theo

Mia

Why aren't viruses considered alive? They clearly do stuff!

Grandpa Theo

A living thing can convert substances, grow, and reproduce on its own. A virus can't do any of that by itself - it has no metabolism of its own. Outside a cell, it's basically just a dead package of genetic material and protein.

Mia

So how does it multiply if it can't do that itself?

Grandpa Theo

It docks onto a body cell and injects its genetic material inside. Then it basically hijacks the cell's factory: without realizing it, the cell manufactures thousands of new viruses until it eventually bursts and releases them.

Mia

And how does a vaccine help against that?

Grandpa Theo

A vaccine shows your immune system harmless parts of the virus, like its docking proteins. Your body then practices making matching antibodies ahead of time - so if it later meets the real virus, it can react much faster.

Not only harmful

There are also viruses that specifically attack bacteria (bacteriophages) - researchers are testing them as a possible replacement for antibiotics when bacteria have become resistant.

Viruses have probably existed for as long as cells themselves - some researchers even believe part of our own genome originally came from ancient virus infections.
