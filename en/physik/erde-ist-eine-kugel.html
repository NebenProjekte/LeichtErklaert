Why the Earth Is a Sphere and Not a Flat Disc

You do not have to take it on faith that the Earth is a sphere — you can check it yourself. As far back as antiquity there were several independent pieces of evidence for it, and none of them needs a spaceship.

Circumference of the Earth ≈ 40,075 km · radius ≈ 6,371 km Eratosthenes determined the circumference around 240 BC with a stick and a shadow
Key terms

Horizon — the limit of sight caused by the curvature of the Earth.
Zenith — the point directly above the observer.
Culmination — the highest position of the sun during the day.
Geoid — the actual shape of the Earth: a sphere flattened at the poles.
Flattening — the diameter at the equator is about 43 km larger than the diameter through the poles.

Five pieces of evidence anyone can follow

Observation and conclusion
ObservationWhat follows from it
Ships disappear hull first and mast lastthe surface curves
The Earth's shadow on the moon is round at every lunar eclipseonly a sphere casts a round shadow from every direction
Different stars are in the sky over southern Spain than over Norwaythe line of sight changes with location
The sun rises earlier in the east than in the westtime zones are only needed on a sphere
You can see farther from a tower than from the groundthe horizon is a curvature limit, not fog

How Eratosthenes measured the circumference

In Syene the sun stood straight overhead at the summer solstice — no shadow.
In Alexandria a stick cast a shadow of 7.2° at the same time.

7.2° is 1/50 of a full circle.
Distance Syene–Alexandria ≈ 800 km

Circumference = 50 · 800 km = 40,000 km

Today's value is 40,075 km. One stick, one shadow and a simple proportion — the error is less than one percent.

Polaris tells you your latitude

Polaris, the North Star, stands almost exactly above the Earth's North Pole. Its height above the horizon therefore corresponds roughly to the observer's geographic latitude: at the North Pole it is directly overhead (90°), at the equator it sits on the horizon (0°). In Palma de Mallorca (about 40° north) you measure roughly 40°, in Hamburg (about 54° north) roughly 54°, in Tromsø (about 70° north) roughly 70°.

In the Southern Hemisphere Polaris can no longer be seen at all; other constellations appear there instead, such as the Southern Cross. Both facts follow naturally from a sphere: anyone travelling south tilts their horizon against the stars until the North Star disappears behind the curve of the Earth. Anyone who travels south and watches the sky along the way can check this evidence for themselves.

How strongly the Earth curves away

Measured from a horizontal line of sight, the Earth's surface drops away by the rule of thumb d² ÷ (2 · R), with R = 6,371 km.
After 1 km: 1 ÷ 12,742 km = 0.08 m, so about 8 cm.
After 10 km: 100 ÷ 12,742 km = 0.00785 km, so about 7.9 m.
After 100 km: 10,000 ÷ 12,742 km = 0.785 km, so about 785 m.
Because the value grows with the square of the distance, the curvature is invisible over short stretches and impossible to miss over long ones. This drop is exactly why the hull of a distant ship disappears first.

💬 Mia asks Grandpa Theo

Mia

Why does the Earth look so flat from the ground, then?

Grandpa Theo

Because it is enormous. On a ten-kilometer hike it curves away by just under eight meters — you simply cannot see that.

Mia

And the horizon?

Grandpa Theo

That is the proof. On a flat disc there would be no fixed limit of sight — with good binoculars you could see as far as you liked.

Common mistakes

"The Earth is a perfect sphere." It is flattened at the poles; the diameter at the equator is about 43 km larger.
The horizon is just haze. Even in perfect visibility the limit remains — it depends only on the height of your eyes.
Columbus proved it. The spherical shape of the Earth had been known since antiquity; the argument was about its size, and there Columbus was wrong.
Naming photos as the only evidence. All five observations above work without a camera.

Practice question

Calculate how far you can see with your eyes 1.70 m above the sea. Approximation: d ≈ 3.57 km · √h (h in meters). Explain why a lighthouse can still be seen from much farther away.

Show solution

d = 3.57 · √1.70 = 3.57 · 1.30 = 4.7 km

A lighthouse 40 m tall has a range of 3.57 · √40 = 22.6 km of its own. The two distances add up because the lines of sight meet at the horizon: 4.7 + 22.6 = about 27 km.
That is exactly why lighthouses are built tall and lookout platforms high up — every meter of height pushes the horizon farther back. On a flat Earth, height would not matter.

The flattening comes from the Earth's rotation: at the equator inertia acts most strongly and stretches the body outwards. All rotating planets show this — on fast-spinning Saturn it is even visible through an amateur telescope.
