Sports Theory - Upper Secondary

Strength endurance explained simply

Strength endurance is the resistance to fatigue during long-lasting or repeated force efforts. Sports science sets a lower limit here: only from about 30% of maximal strength does one speak of strength endurance - below that it is general endurance.

Strength endurance thus depends on two quantities at once: on maximal strength (the higher it is, the lower the relative share per repetition) and on the energy supply.

relative load = force applied ÷ maximal strength Whoever becomes stronger automatically lowers the relative load at the same resistance

The three energy systems

The systems do not take turns, they overlap. What matters is which one is currently supplying the largest share.

A rule to remember for the exam: flow rate and capacity behave in opposite ways. Energy that is quickly available is scarce; energy that is plentiful arrives slowly.

Fatigue: why it is really over

The widespread explanation "lactate makes the muscle acidic and it becomes over-acidified" is wrong in this form and is marked as a mistake in exams. What is precise:

  • In anaerobic glycolysis, lactate and hydrogen ions are formed separately from each other. It is not the lactate that lowers the pH but the H+ ions.
  • Acidosis inhibits key enzymes of glycolysis and disturbs calcium binding at troponin - the contraction becomes weaker.
  • At least as important is the accumulation of inorganic phosphate, which directly impairs the force development of the cross-bridges.
  • Lactate itself is a fuel. Via the lactate shuttle it is used again in the heart muscle, in slow fibres and in the liver.

The adaptations differ fundamentally from maximal strength training. Instead of cross-section and activation, the following improve:

  • Capillary density - shorter diffusion distances for oxygen
  • Number and size of mitochondria - higher oxidative capacity
  • Enzyme activity of the oxidative metabolic pathways
  • Buffering capacity - the muscle tolerates more H+ before it breaks down
  • Glycogen stores - larger reserves in the muscle

Worked example: why maximal strength improves endurance

Task: 20 repetitions with 50 kg

Before – maximal strength 80 kg:
relative load = 50 ÷ 80 = 63% → very demanding

After strength training – maximal strength 110 kg:
relative load = 50 ÷ 110 = 45% → clearly easier

Without a single endurance stimulus, the same set is now easier. This is why every strength endurance concept also contains a maximal strength component.

Key terms
Strength endurance
Resistance to fatigue during force efforts above about 30% of maximal strength.
Flow rate
Amount of energy per unit of time that a system can deliver.
Capacity
Total amount of energy that a system can provide.
Anaerobic threshold
Highest load at which lactate formation and removal are still in balance.
Lactate shuttle
Transport of lactate to tissues that oxidise it as fuel.
Buffering capacity
Ability of the muscle to capture H+ ions before the pH falls critically.
Common exam mistake

"Lactate over-acidifies the muscle and causes sore muscles." Two mistakes in a single sentence. The acidosis is due to the H+ ions, not to the lactate anion. And muscle soreness is caused by microscopic structural damage, mainly after eccentric loading - it appears hours later, whereas lactate is cleared within an hour.

Presenting the systems as a relay race. They work overlapping, not one after the other. The question is always which system is currently dominant.

How to phrase it in an exam

"At a load duration of about 40 seconds, anaerobic glycolysis dominates. Its high flow rate allows fast ATP resynthesis, but its limited capacity leads to the accumulation of hydrogen ions. The resulting acidosis inhibits key enzymes of glycolysis and impairs calcium binding, which shows up as a drop in performance."

Exam question

A 400-metre runner slows down clearly over the last 80 metres. Explain the drop in performance on the basis of the energy supply and evaluate which training method provides a remedy.

Show expected answer

The answer should assign the load duration of about 45 to 50 seconds to anaerobic glycolysis. Its capacity is limited; with increasing duration, H+ ions and inorganic phosphate accumulate. The acidosis inhibits phosphofructokinase and disturbs calcium binding at troponin, so that fewer cross-bridges are formed - the force per stride falls, the stride frequency collapses. Aerobic oxidation cannot close the gap, since its flow rate is too low. The training method to derive is intensive strength endurance or lactate tolerance training, which raises the buffering capacity; in addition, aerobic base training to speed up lactate removal during the rests. For the evaluation, it should be justified why pure base training alone is not sufficient.
