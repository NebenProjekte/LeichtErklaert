Sports Theory - Training & Muscle

Hypertrophy explained simply

Hypertrophy means growth in thickness. Training does not give your muscles new fibres - the existing ones get thicker. And that does not happen in the gym, it happens afterwards, during recovery.

Stimulus → recovery → adaptation Leave out one of the three and nothing happens

The stimulus is an unfamiliar load. The adaptation is the reserve the body then builds, so that the same load demands less of it next time.

💬 Mia & Grandpa Theo

Mia

Do muscles grow during training?

Grandpa Theo

No, during training they first get weaker. You are only setting the stimulus. The building happens afterwards - while you eat and above all while you sleep.

Mia

Then could I just train hard every day and keep setting new stimuli?

Grandpa Theo

Then you set stimuli without ever finishing the adaptation. That is like tearing down a house every morning before it is done. Rest days are not doing nothing, they are the building phase.

For the building material the body needs protein, and for the building site it needs energy from cellular respiration. That is why growth depends as much on eating and sleeping as on training.

Fun Fact

Anyone who has built muscle once gets it back faster after a break. The reason: while growing, the fibre takes on additional cell nuclei - and those remain even when the muscle shrinks again. That is the biological basis of „muscle memory“.
