Area and Volume Formulas Explained Simply

Whether it's a circle, sphere or cylinder - the formulas for area and volume are more closely related than you might think. All three only need the radius r (and for the cylinder, also the height h).

Circle area: A = π × r²
Sphere volume: V = 4⁄3 × π × r³
Cylinder volume: V = π × r² × h

💬 Mia asks Grandpa Theo

Mia

Why does a sphere have almost the same formula as a circle, just with r³ instead of r²?

Grandpa Theo

Because a sphere is basically a 3D circle! For an area (2D) you count r × r, for a volume (3D) you simply add one more dimension: r × r × r.

Mia

And the cylinder?

Grandpa Theo

A cylinder is just a circle “stretched” upward by the height h - that's why its volume is circle area times height: π × r² × h.

Mia

Show me a worked example!

Grandpa Theo

A soccer ball has a radius of about 11cm. Its volume is then 4⁄3 × π × 11³ ≈ 5575 cm³ - that's over 5.5 liters of air!

Try it yourself

A can has r = 3.5cm and h = 12cm. How much fits inside?
Answer: V = π × 3.5² × 12 ≈ 462 cm³

π (Pi) is approximately 3.14159 - the formulas are exact only for perfect circles, spheres and cylinders; real objects are always just approximations of them.
