Exponential Growth Explained Simply

With linear growth, the same amount is added each time. With exponential growth, the amount is multiplied by the same factor each time - and that gets surprisingly huge, surprisingly fast.

K(n) = K₀ × (1 + p÷100)ⁿ K₀ = starting value, p = growth rate in %, n = number of steps

💬 Mia asks Grandpa Theo

Mia

How fast is exponential growth, really?

Grandpa Theo

Look at the bacteria above: they double every 20 minutes. Starting from 1, after just 4 doublings (80 minutes) you already have 16 - and after 24 hours it would be billions!

Mia

Wow. Does that apply to money too?

Grandpa Theo

Exactly, that's called compound interest: if you invest $1000 at 5% interest, you get $50 in the first year. But in the second year you get 5% on $1050, so a bit more than the first year.

Mia

Does that really add up that much?

Grandpa Theo

Yes! After 10 years, your $1000 would have grown to around $1629 - just from interest continuously earning interest on itself.

Try it yourself

$1000 at 5% interest, after 10 years: K(10) = 1000 × 1.05¹⁰ ≈ $1628.89
Check it with your calculator!

The same principle - just reversed, as a decrease instead of an increase - also explains why radioactive substances decay more and more slowly (see Radioactivity).
